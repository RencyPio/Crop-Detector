**Summary:** Early blight of tomato is caused by *Alternaria solani* and appears as brown spots with concentric rings on leaves and fruit. It can spread quickly in warm, humid, rainy conditions and may cause serious yield loss if not managed. [1]

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Remove and destroy all crop debris after harvest to reduce inoculum. [1]  
- Treat seeds with *Trichoderma asperellum* at 4 g per kg of seed before planting. [1]  
- Practice crop rotation, avoiding tomato or related crops for 2–3 years. [5]  
- Keep the field well‑drained and avoid overhead irrigation that leaves foliage wet. [4]  

**Chemical options:**  
- Spray Iprodione 50 % WP at 600 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Azoxystrobin 23 % SC at 200 ml per acre. [1]  
- Spray Mancozeb 35 % SC at 1 kg per acre. [1]  
- Spray Metiram 70 % WG at 1.0 kg per acre. [1]  
- Spray Pyraclostrobin 20 % WG at 200 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Zineb 75 % WP at 800 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Azoxystrobin 18.2 % + Difenoconazole 11.4 % SC at 200 ml per acre. [1]  
- Spray Metiram 55 % + Pyraclostrobin 5 % WG at 600 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Tebuconazole 50 % + Trifloxystrobin 25 % WG at 140 g per acre. [1]  

**When to get expert help:**  
- Disease spreads rapidly despite cultural measures.  
- Fungicide applications are needed repeatedly or at critical growth stages.  
- You are unsure about correct mixing, equipment calibration, or safety precautions.  
- Symptoms worsen after treatments.
