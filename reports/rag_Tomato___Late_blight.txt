**Summary:** Tomato late blight is caused by *Phytophthora infestans* and can quickly destroy leaves, stems and fruit, leading to severe loss if not managed promptly. [1]

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Plant early to avoid peak disease pressure. [2]  
- Keep foliage dry; avoid sprinkler irrigation and remove water from leaves promptly. [2]  
- Scout fields regularly and remove any infected plants, fruit, weeds or volunteer nightshades. [2][5]  
- Use disease‑resistant tomato varieties where available. [2][5]  
- Apply soil‑borne biocontrol *Trichoderma asperellum* at 1 kg per acre before planting. [1]

**Chemical options:**  
- Spray Cyazofamid 34.5 % SC at 80 ml per acre. [1]  
- Spray Azoxystrobin 23 % SC at 200 ml per acre. [1]  
- Spray Mancozeb 35 % SC at 1000 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Zineb 75 % WP at 800 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Azoxystrobin 18.2 % + Difenoconazole 11.4 % SC at 200 ml per acre. [1]

**When to get expert help:**  
- Disease appears despite cultural measures and initial fungicide applications. [5]  
- You need assistance selecting resistant varieties suited to your area. [2]  
- Weather forecasts predict prolonged wet conditions and you are unsure about timing of protectant sprays. [5]

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
