Tomato___Late_blight
mode: llm

**Summary:** Tomato late blight, caused by *Phytophthora infestans*, produces water‑soaked black lesions that expand rapidly and can destroy leaves and fruit. It spreads quickly in cool, wet conditions and can cause severe loss if not managed. [1][5]

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Apply soil‑incorporated *Trichoderma asperellum* (viride) at 1.0 kg per acre. [1]  
- Plant early in the season to avoid peak disease pressure. [2]  
- Remove and destroy any infected plants, fruits, weeds, and volunteer tomato or potato plants nearby. [2][4][5]  
- Avoid sprinkler or overhead irrigation; use drip or furrow irrigation instead. [2][4][5]  
- Ensure transplants are disease‑free before planting. [4][5]  

**Chemical options:**  
- Spray Cyazofamid 34.5 % SC at 80 ml per acre. [1]  
- Spray Azoxystrobin 23 % SC at 200 ml per acre. [1]  
- Apply Mancozeb 35 % SC at 1000 g per acre. [1]  
- Apply Zineb 75 % WP at 800 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Azoxystrobin 18.2 % + Difenoconazole 11.4 % SC at 200 ml per acre. [1]  

**When to get expert help:**  
- Disease appears despite cultural measures and first fungicide application.  
- Rapid spread under favorable weather (high humidity, 18‑22 °C).  
- Uncertainty about correct product mixing or application equipment.

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
