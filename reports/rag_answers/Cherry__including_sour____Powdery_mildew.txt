Cherry_(including_sour)___Powdery_mildew
mode: llm

**Summary:** Powdery mildew appears as white, web‑like growth on cherry leaves, shoots and fruit and can cause serious crop loss, especially on new growth in warm, humid conditions [1].

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Prune out and destroy infected shoots, water sprouts and any buds showing mildew [1].  
- Spray as soon as petal fall occurs and repeat 2–3 weeks later if mildew is still present [1].  
- Avoid using the same fungicide or those with similar chemistry more than twice per year to limit resistance [1].  

**Chemical options:**  
- Apply wettable sulfur at 10–20 lb/acre; re‑enter after 24 h and do not spray within 3 weeks of oil applications [4].  
- Use potassium bicarbonate (Kaligreen) at 2.5–3 lb/acre; re‑enter after 4 h [4].  
- Apply azoxystrobin/difenconazole (Quadris Top) at 12–14 fl oz/acre; REI 12 h, PHI 0 days; rotate with a different FRAC group after use [3].  
- Apply azoxystrobin/propiconazole (QuiltXcel) at 14 fl oz/acre; REI 12 h, PHI 0 days; rotate with a different FRAC group after use [3].  
- Apply tebuconazole/trifloxystrobin (Adament 50WG) at 4–8 fl oz/acre; REI 120 h (5 days), PHI 1 day; rotate with a different FRAC group after use [3].  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If mildew spreads rapidly despite two sprays.  
- If fruit shows heavy infection that could affect market quality.  
- If you are unsure about mixing or timing of fungicides.

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
