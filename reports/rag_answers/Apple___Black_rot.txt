**Summary:** Black rot is a fungal disease of apples that can cause leaf loss, fruit rot, and limb cankers, leading to reduced tree vigor and unmarketable fruit[4]. It is serious if not managed, especially in warm, humid conditions[4].  

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Remove and destroy mummified fruit, dead trees, and infected limbs to reduce inoculum[1].  
- Prune out cankers and dead wood; burn or chop prunings and keep the orchard clear of debris[2].  
- Sanitize tools and avoid leaving pruning piles on the orchard perimeter[2].  

**Chemical options:**  
- Apply copper‑based protectant sprays early in the season[1].  
- Use lime‑sulfur sprays as a protectant early in the season[1].  
- Use Daconil (chlorothalonil) as a protectant early in the season[1].  
- In commercial orchards, apply ethylene bisdithiocarbamate (EBDC) fungicides such as Polyram, Manzate, or Dithane[1].  
- After petal fall, apply Captan at full rate[1].  
- Use a combination of Benlate and Captan after petal fall for additional control[1].  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If disease pressure remains high despite sanitation and protectant sprays.  
- If you observe extensive limb cankers or rapid defoliation.  
- If fruit rot continues into harvest, affecting marketability.

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
