Tomato___Bacterial_spot
mode: llm

**Summary:** Tomato bacterial spot is caused by *Xanthomonas axonopodis* and produces small brown water‑soaked spots with yellow halos on leaves and fruit; it can lead to defoliation and yield loss if not managed promptly. [1][2]  

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Use disease‑free seed or certified disease‑free transplants to prevent introduction of the pathogen. [2][3]  
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris and any volunteer tomato plants after harvest. [1][2]  
- Avoid sprinkler irrigation; use drip irrigation or water at the base of plants to keep foliage dry. [2][3]  
- Rotate tomato with a non‑host crop and keep weed hosts such as nightshade away from the field. [2][3]  

**Chemical options:**  
- Apply Streptocycline at 40–100 ppm (equivalent to 20 g per acre) as a spray; follow label for timing and repeat as needed. [1]  
- Use a copper‑based protectant (e.g., copper hydroxide/Kocide 3000) at 0.75–1.75 lb per acre; apply at first sign of disease and repeat every 10–14 days during warm, moist conditions. This recommendation is from the USA. [5]  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If disease spreads rapidly despite cultural measures and the first spray. [2]  
- If you are unsure about the correct product label rates or safety intervals. [5]  
- If you need assistance selecting a copper product that is approved for organic production. [5]

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
