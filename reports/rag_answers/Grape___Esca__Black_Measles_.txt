Grape___Esca_(Black_Measles)
mode: llm

**Summary:** Esca (Black Measles) is a trunk disease of grapevines that causes interveinal leaf striping, fruit spots, and can lead to sudden shoot dieback (apoplexy) [1][3].

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Prune vines during dormancy and seal pruning wounds promptly, as wounds are infection sites for several weeks after pruning [1].  
- Remove and destroy heavily infected wood, canes, or entire vines that show severe symptoms, because the pathogen becomes a permanent wood infection that cannot be cured with chemicals [1].  
- Maintain good vineyard sanitation by clearing fallen leaves and debris that may harbor fungal structures [1].  
- Avoid excessive irrigation and ensure good canopy airflow to reduce humidity that favors fungal growth [1].

**Chemical options:**  
- Not found in the knowledge base.  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If you observe sudden shoot dieback (apoplexy) or extensive wood decay despite cultural measures [1][3].  
- When you are unable to identify or properly remove infected wood.  
- If disease symptoms spread rapidly across the vineyard.

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
