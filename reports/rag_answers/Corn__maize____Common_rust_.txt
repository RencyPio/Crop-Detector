Corn_(maize)___Common_rust_
mode: llm

**Summary:** Common rust of maize is caused by *Puccinia sorghi* and appears as brown‑red pustules on leaves; it can spread to sheaths and stalks and reduce yield if severe [1].

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Remove and destroy alternate hosts such as Oxalis species from the field [1].  
- Collect crop residues after harvest and destroy them by burning or burying to reduce inoculum [1].  

**Chemical options:**  
- Foliar spray of kresoxim‑methyl 44.3 % SC at 1 ml per litre of water, applied at 35 days after sowing (DAS) [1].  
- Foliar spray of tebuconazole at 1 ml per litre of water, applied at 35 DAS [1].  
- Foliar spray of chlorothalonil at 2 ml per litre of water, applied at 35 DAS [1].  
- Foliar spray of mancozeb at 2 ml per litre of water, applied at 35 DAS [1].  
- Repeat the same spray (any of the above products) at 50 DAS [1].  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If rust spreads rapidly to sheaths, husks or stalks despite the above sprays [1].  
- If you are unable to locate or remove alternate host plants [1].  
- If you observe severe leaf death or yield loss and need advice on resistant hybrids [2].

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
