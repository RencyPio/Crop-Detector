**Summary:** Cedar‑apple rust is a fungal disease that needs both apple trees and nearby eastern red cedars to complete its cycle; it causes orange‑yellow leaf lesions and can reduce fruit marketability, but it is manageable with cultural practices and, if needed, fungicide programs [1][4].

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**
- Plant resistant apple cultivars such as ‘Baldwin’, ‘Empire’, ‘Enterprise’, ‘Gala Supreme’, ‘Liberty’, ‘Regent’, ‘Spartan’, ‘Viking’, etc. [3]  
- Remove or destroy wild or unwanted eastern red cedars that have rust galls near the orchard. [2][3]  
- Prune apple trees to open the canopy for better light, air flow, and spray penetration. [3]  
- Scout for cedar galls in May‑June and for leaf lesions on apples in mid‑summer; monitor weather for wet periods that favor spore release. [3][5]  
- Apply protectant fungicides at bloom if rust was seen the previous year and weather is warm and wet; make follow‑up sprays as indicated by scouting and weather until mid‑season when cedar galls stop producing spores. [3]  

**Chemical options:**  
- Not found in the knowledge base.  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If rust symptoms appear despite cultural controls and scouting.  
- If you need specific fungicide recommendations, rates, or timing for your region.  
- When cedar galls are abundant and difficult to manage manually.

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
