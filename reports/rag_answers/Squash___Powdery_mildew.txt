Squash___Powdery_mildew
mode: llm

**Summary:** Powdery mildew on squash appears as white, fluffy growth on leaves and stems and can reduce photosynthesis and yield if not managed promptly. It is a common and serious disease in humid conditions. [1]

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Plant resistant squash varieties if available. [2]  
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris and weeds that can harbor the fungus. [2]  
- Space plants to improve air circulation and reduce leaf wetness. [2]  
- Monitor regularly from the vegetative stage through fruit development and apply controls at the first sign of symptoms. [2]

**Chemical options:**  
- **Cinnamaldehyde (Cinnacure)** – Apply 0.25–1 gal per acre; re‑enter after 4 hours; no pre‑harvest interval. Make no more than two consecutive applications before rotating to a different mode of action. [3][4] (USA)  
- **Potassium bicarbonate (Kaligreen)** – Apply 2.5–5 lb per acre; re‑enter after 4 hours; 1‑day pre‑harvest interval. Use the higher rate when disease pressure is severe. [4] (USA)  
- **Micronized sulfur (Microthiol)** – Apply at the label‑specified rate; re‑enter after 24 hours; no pre‑harvest interval. Avoid use on sulfur‑sensitive varieties and when temperatures exceed 95 °F. [4] (USA)  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If disease spreads rapidly despite cultural measures and the first fungicide application.  
- If you need assistance selecting a resistant squash variety for your region.  
- If you observe phytotoxic injury after applying any product.

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
