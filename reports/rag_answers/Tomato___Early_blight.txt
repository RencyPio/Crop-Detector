Tomato___Early_blight
mode: llm

**Summary:** Early blight of tomato is caused by *Alternaria solani*. It produces brown concentric rings on leaves and fruit and can spread quickly in warm, humid conditions, so it can become serious if not managed. [1]

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Remove and destroy all crop debris after harvest. [1]  
- Treat seeds with *Trichoderma asperellum* at 4 g per kg of seed before planting. [1]  
- Practice crop rotation, avoiding tomato or nightshade crops for 2–3 years. [5]  
- Keep the field well‑drained and avoid overhead irrigation that leaves foliage wet. [4]  

**Chemical options:**  
- Spray Iprodione 50 % WP at 600 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Azoxystrobin 23 % SC at 200 ml per acre. [1]  
- Spray Mancozeb 35 % SC at 1 kg per acre. [1]  
- Spray Metiram 70 % WG at 1.0 kg per acre. [1]  
- Spray Pyraclostrobin 20 % WG at 200 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Zineb 75 % WP at 800 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Azoxystrobin 18.2 % + Difenoconazole 11.4 % SC at 200 ml per acre. [1]  
- Spray Metiram 55 % + Pyraclostrobin 5 % WG at 600 g per acre. [1]  
- Spray Tebuconazole 50 % + Trifloxystrobin 25 % WG at 140 g per acre. [1]  

**When to get expert help:**  
- Disease continues to spread despite cultural measures and the above sprays. [5]  
- You need help identifying resistant varieties suitable for your area. [2]  
- Severe rain and humidity make disease control difficult; seek advice on timing of additional applications. [4]

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
