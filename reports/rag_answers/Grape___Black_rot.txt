Grape___Black_rot
mode: llm

**Summary:** Black rot is a serious fungal disease of grapes that can cause complete crop loss if not managed early in the season [3].

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Remove and destroy diseased leaves, tendrils, canes, and fruit as soon as they appear [5].  
- Prune out mummified canes during dormancy and bury them to reduce inoculum [5].  
- Plant grapes in a sunny site with good air flow; orient rows with prevailing winds to dry foliage quickly [5].  
- Thin the canopy by removing excess shoots or leaves to lower humidity around the vines [1].

**Chemical options:**  
- Apply protectant fungicides such as sulfur (Bordeaux mix), copper, or mancozeb shortly before bloom and repeat at the intervals listed on the product label until about 4–6 weeks after bloom [1].  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If you see many infected leaves or clusters despite cultural measures, contact a local extension officer for a spray program review [2].  
- If disease develops rapidly in warm, humid conditions and threatens the entire crop, seek professional advice immediately [3].

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
