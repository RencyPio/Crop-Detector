Corn_(maize)___Northern_Leaf_Blight
mode: llm

**Summary:** Northern leaf blight of maize is caused by fungal pathogens that create long cigar‑shaped lesions on lower leaves; it can spread quickly in wet, cool weather and reduce yield if not managed. [1]

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Burn or bury infected maize stubble to reduce inoculum. [1]  
- Practice timely tillage to break down residue and lower disease carry‑over. [3]  

**Chemical options:**  
- Spray mancozeb or zineb at 2–4 g per liter of water. [1]  
- Spray propiconazole 25 % EC at 1 ml per liter of water. [1]  
- Apply these sprays at 35 days after sowing (DAS) and repeat at 50 DAS. [1]  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If disease continues to spread despite cultural measures and the recommended sprays. [Not found in the knowledge base.]  
- If you need assistance selecting resistant hybrid varieties or adjusting spray timing for local conditions. [Not found in the knowledge base.]

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
