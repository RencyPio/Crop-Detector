**Summary:** Strawberry leaf scorch is a fungal disease that creates brown‑to‑purple spots on leaves, can scorch foliage, and reduces fruit yield and quality. It can become serious if wet conditions and overhead irrigation favor spread[2].  

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Increase air circulation by planting in sunny, open sites and avoid crowding[4].  
- Use drip or furrow irrigation instead of overhead sprinklers and water in the morning so foliage dries quickly[1].  
- Remove all strawberry plant debris at the end of the growing season and dispose of it away from future plantings[1][4].  
- Plant strawberries as annuals on raised beds or berms to reduce overwintering of the fungus[4].  
- Control weeds to improve drying of leaves[5].  

**Chemical options:**  
- Not found in the knowledge base.  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If leaf scorch continues despite cultural practices.  
- If fruit shows severe discoloration or sunken lesions.  
- When you need specific fungicide dose and spray schedule for your region.

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
