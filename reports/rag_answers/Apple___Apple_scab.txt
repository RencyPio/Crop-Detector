**Summary:** Apple scab is caused by *Venturia inaequalis* and can severely distort leaves and fruit, reducing yield if not managed. [1]

**What to do now (cultural / non‑chemical):**  
- Remove and destroy fallen leaves and pruned material during winter to break the disease cycle. [1]  
- Apply 5 % urea to the orchard before leaf fall in autumn to hasten leaf decomposition. [1]  
- Apply 2 % urea before bud break to further speed leaf breakdown. [1]  

**Chemical options:**  
- Spray Tridemorph 0.1 % before flowering. [1]  
- Spray Mancozeb 0.25 % at the bearing stage. [1]  
- At pink bud (≈15 days after first spray), spray Captan 250 g / 100 L or Mancozeb 300 g / 100 L. [1][2]  
- At petal fall, spray Carbendazim 50 g / 100 L. [1][2]  
- Ten days later, spray Captan 200 g / 100 L or Mancozeb 300 g / 100 L. [1][2]  
- Fourteen days after fruit set, spray Captafol 150 g / 100 L with a sticker (teepol or triton 6 ml per 10 L spray). [1][2]  

**When to get expert help:**  
- If disease symptoms persist despite following the above schedule. [1]  
- If you notice severe leaf distortion or heavy fruit infection early in the season. [1]  
- If you need assistance selecting appropriate stickers or mixing rates. [1]

Always follow the pesticide label and consult your local agriculture officer before applying any chemical.
